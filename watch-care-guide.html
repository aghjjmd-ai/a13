The Watch Care Guide

A well-looked-after watch can run for decades. Most of the care takes a few minutes a month.

01 // Winding and power reserve

Automatic watches wind as you move, but desk days may not be enough to keep them full. If yours stops overnight, give it 20–40 turns of the crown in the morning. Manual-wind watches should be wound at the same time each day, gently, until you feel resistance; never force it.

02 // Cleaning

  • Case and bracelet: warm water, mild soap and a soft toothbrush, if water resistance allows.
  • Leather straps: wipe with a dry cloth; let them air between wears; keep away from water and perfume.
  • Rubber straps: rinse in fresh water after the pool or sea.
  • Crystal: microfibre cloth only.

03 // Magnetism

Phones, laptop lids, speakers, bag clasps and induction hobs can magnetise a mechanical movement, making it suddenly run very fast. Keep your watch a hand’s width away from strong magnets. A watchmaker can demagnetise a watch in minutes.

04 // Water and temperature

AvoidWhy
Hot showers and saunasHeat and steam stress the seals
Operating the crown underwaterWater can enter instantly
Sudden hot-to-cold changesCan cause condensation under the crystal
Leaving in direct sunFades dials and dries out lubricants

05 // Service intervals

Many makers recommend a full service every five to ten years for mechanical watches. Book sooner if the rate changes a lot, the power reserve drops, the crown feels gritty or you see moisture. Have water resistance tested every year or two if you swim with it.

06 // Storage

Store watches dial-up in a soft-lined box, away from magnets and damp. A watch you will not wear for weeks can simply be left to stop; restart it with a few turns of the crown and reset the time and date safely. The Power Reserve Tracker shows whether it will still be running.
